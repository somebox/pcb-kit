# Where each piece came from

Inventory taken 2026-10-10. "same" means the copies in the sign (rp2354-messages-sign), siebenuhr (controller-v2)
and spotlight (rgb-spotlight) repos were byte-identical; the kit version is the merge of the variants.

| Kit | Source | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pcbkit/konnect.py` | `scripts/kmcp.py` (same in all three) | binary overridable with `KONNECT_BIN` |
| `pcbkit/schematic.py` | `scripts/builder.py` (three variants) | merged: sign's base, siebenuhr's `P2`, `glob` labels and `mark_dnp`, spotlight's `unit` and reference dedupe. `P` and `POWER` set with `configure()` instead of editing the module |

| `pcbkit/checks/erc.py` | spotlight `scripts/erc.py` (kicad-cli) | the sign/siebenuhr `erc.py` used konnect's `run_erc`; same information |
| `pcbkit/checks/netlist.py` | `scripts/netcheck.py` (sign/siebenuhr same, spotlight richer) | the parser and the `expect`/`same` helpers; each project's assertions stay in the project |
| `pcbkit/checks/route_check.py` | dodeca `tools/route_check.py` | power classes default to classes with track width >= 0.5 mm |

| `pcbkit/release/previews.py` | `scripts/make_previews.sh` (sign/spotlight, differ only in board list and render zoom) | Python; zoom and render size are flags |
| `pcbkit/release/plot_print.py` | `scripts/plot_print.py` (same) | LED-reference hiding is now `--hide-refs REGEX` |
| `pcbkit/release/jlc_fab.py` | sign `scripts/jlc_fab.py` + `scripts/jlc/corrections.csv` | corrections table in `pcbkit/data/jlc/`; the BOM also leaves out parts excluded from position files, so it lists exactly what the CPL places, and the summary counts the hand-assembled parts (2026-10-15, synth-blocks analog-voice) |
| `pcbkit/release/panel.py` | sign `scripts/make_panel.sh` + `setup_tools.sh` | KiKit pinned to the same commit, installed once under `~/.local/share/pcbkit`; presets, `edge_tabs.py` and `panel_post.py` in `pcbkit/data/panel/`, referenced by absolute path through a generated override preset; rows is a flag. 2026-10-18: `--hide-refs` reaches the panel PDF too (the sign's old make_panel.sh hid its LED references there through plot_print.py; the first harvest dropped that) |

First used on the ESP32 HA protoboard (2026-10-10) |
| `pcbkit/sexp.py` | dodeca `tools/sexp.py` | small s-expression helpers used by the dodeca generators; kept for the next harvest |

| `pcbkit/spice.py` | dodeca `hardware/control-board/v1.3/sim/spice.py` | same idea (scratch directory, `-D ngbehavior=psa`, @SIM@/@VENDOR@ placeholders, measurements and wrdata files); adds an `aborted` flag from ngspice's own messages, an optional gear-integration retry and the `pcbkit spice` CLI. The decks and averaged models stay in dodeca |
| `pcbkit/route.py` | `scripts/route_strip.py` of rp2354-messages-sign and rgb-spotlight (identical but for spotlight's round outline) + the plane-via, neck-down and track-end audit steps of the sign's `scripts/route_controller.py` | the generic part only: `Copper`, `Grid`, `astar_ml`, `complete_nets`, `tie_pads`, `stitch_gnd`, `escape_vias` (pins now required), `chamfer`; `plane_vias`, `neckdown` (fine-pitch footprints a parameter), `audit_track_ends`, and a `writer(board)` for the track/via helpers. Added: polygon outlines (edge distance cached per cell: uncached, a 156-point outline made a run 3.5 h instead of 2 min), polygon keep-outs tested against the copper edge, not the centre line (`keepout_hit` for hand-placed copper too; `plane_vias` honours them and the outline), copper layers taken from the board instead of F/In1/In2/B, per-layer cost (`LAYER_COST`). Fixed: `HWEIGHT` reached `astar_ml` but was never applied. Dropped: the unused single-layer `astar` (its heuristic used an undefined name). The LED-strip fan-in (`lane_depths`, `fanin`, the escape pin lists) stays in the sign repo. First used on esp32-s3-ha-node (2026-10-12) |
| `pcbkit/route.py`: `pad_item`, `snap_to_pad_centres`, `HOLE_CLEARANCE` | Orbitchron `scripts/route_extras.py` (2026-10-14) | pads at an angle as capsules along their long side, widened just enough to cover the corners (short side / 2 x (sqrt 2 - 1) + 0.01, less for round-rect corners; a fixed 0.09 under-covered 0.65 mm pads): the old bounding boxes of SSOP pins at 30 degrees overlapped their neighbours, so the pins were unreachable (Orbitchron driver, the nets of its two angled chips: 8 connections routed before, 46 after; failed nets 35 before, 6 after; no gap below the routing clearance). Inscribed capsules for connectivity and route ends, so routes now end on rotated pad centres themselves. Added beyond route_extras: plated pads are obstacles on every copper layer they reach (they were F/B only, so a route on In1/In2 could cross a barrel), unplated holes on all layers and at `HOLE_CLEARANCE`, round pads as discs. Checked against KiCad's pad polygons on seven boards (siebenuhr controller, spotlight driver and LED, esp32-s3-ha-node, Orbitchron clock, driver and v1 LED board; about 1,550 angled pads): every covering shape contains the pad, every inscribed one lies inside it. 2026-10-18 (PR review): a padstack that differs by layer (front/inner/back or custom) gives one shape per group of layers (`pad_items`), joined as one pad for connectivity; an inscribed capsule of a chamfered pad stays inside the cut corners (it did not above a chamfer ratio of 0.29); `snap_to_pad_centres` tests each extension against other-net copper and necks it down as `complete_nets` does, or leaves the end (on siebenuhr's controller one +3V3 extension had come within 0.2 mm of a LED_DATA track). No corpus pad's shapes changed |
| `pcbkit cleanup --dangling` | Orbitchron `scripts/route_extras.py` `prune_dangling` | rewritten: route_extras removed a segment with `BOARD.Remove()` and kept it if the net then had more pieces, counting without zones, so a stub from a pad into a pour could go. Now planned on the file text by uuid like the rest of cleanup, and only a true leaf goes: one end touching nothing of the net (pour outlines count), every other neighbour meeting at the other end. Agrees with DRC's dangling count on siebenuhr (1), spotlight and esp32-s3-ha-node (0). 2026-10-18: each segment's contacts are found once and only the neighbours of a removed one are checked again (a 200-segment dead end listed from its anchored end: 19 s before, 0.4 s) |
| `pcbkit/placement.py`, `pcbkit place-score`, skill `placement-study` | Orbitchron `scripts/drv_place_score.py` + siebenuhr controller-v2 `scripts/layout/score.py`, `run_variants.sh`, `variant_v*.py` + spotlight `scripts/ratsnest.py` | the method from siebenuhr (score placement variants by ratsnest, route only the winner) with Orbitchron's refinements (crossings only where the sides can meet, plane nets left out, penalty zones, free choices solved per candidate, coordinate descent over groups) as a library and CLI; the study (groups, candidates, zones, free choices) is a Python file in the project. Changed: crossings counted over the whole board, every edge of a net a move touches recomputed (drv_place_score.py missed edges between two unmoved pads); courtyards compared as polygons, not boxes (rotated parts were rejected); through-hole pads block both sides (with their copper on the other side; the board-edge test covers each copper layer's shape of a padstack that differs by layer); via fields as exact polygon areas instead of sampled boxes; `assign()` generalises the gate and GPIO brute force (branch and bound). The ratsnest picture is SVG with crossings circled (spotlight's needed ImageMagick). Orbitchron's gate and GPIO costs come out the same (227.9, 600.6), and its search, started from a deliberately bad placement, returns to the placement and choices drv_place_score.py chose, at about 22 ms a candidate against 100 ms |

## Still in the projects (candidates for the next pass)

- `scripts/route_strip.py` (spotlight): its generic router moved to `pcbkit/route.py` on 2026-10-12 (the 8-line
  divergence was spotlight's round outline, kept). Spotlight still imports its own copy; switch it to `pcbkit.route`
  when next touched. The sign switched on 2026-10-18 and keeps only its LED-strip fan-in.
- `scripts/gen_*_pcb.py`, `sch_*.py`, `mod_*.py`, `ctl_*.py`: board generators. Project data by nature, but they share
  a shape (placement tables, `build()` calls) that a `pcbkit.board` module could carry: the collision-checked spiral
  placement and zone/stackup setup in dodeca's `pcb_build.py`.
- dodeca `tools/pcb_build.py`, `pcb_update.py`, `power_stage.py` (engine parts), `plane_vias.py`, `maze_route.py`,
  `route.py` (Freerouting driver), `assemble.py` (schematic from `design/*.json`), `silkscreen.py`, `guide_images.py`.
- `scripts/make_fab.sh` is still per project (board list, which boards get panels); the DRC gate and the exports are the kit's.
- siebenuhr `scripts/route/` (`route_board.sh` drives KiCadRoutingTools: power copper, guards, gap router, cleanup, GND fill) is the
  first project chain on that router and the model for a `pcbkit route` wrapper; `enclosure_check.py`, `enclosure_heightmap.py`, `placement_plan.py`; spotlight `mech.py`,
  `set_outline.py`; sign `resilk.py`, `renumber_controller.py`, `build_matrix.py`. siebenuhr's `scripts/layout/score.py`, spotlight's
  `ratsnest.py` and Orbitchron's `drv_place_score.py` and `route_extras.py` are covered by `pcbkit.placement`, `pcbkit.route` and
  `pcbkit cleanup --dangling` (2026-10-14); switch them when next touched (siebenuhr's quick maze route per variant has no kit
  counterpart yet).
